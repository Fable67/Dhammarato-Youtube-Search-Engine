### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato and a student (Speaker A):

**Core Problem: Anxiety and "Not Knowing What to Do"**

The student wakes up feeling grumpy, with a slight headache and a sense of unease. He is anxious about having free time and not knowing how to "fill" his day. He feels uncomfortable when he has nothing to do, which Dhammarato identifies as the classic definition of anxiety.

**Key Teachings from Dhammarato:**

1.  **Stop Trying to "Get Out of It":** The student's primary mistake is trying to *fix* or *deal with* the uncomfortable feeling. The instruction is to stop dealing and instead **investigate** the anxiety. Get very close to it, like a camera zooming in, rather than trying to run away from it.

2.  **Use the Breath Immediately:**
    - **Morning Routine:** The very first thing upon waking (and throughout the day) should be conscious, deep breathing (Anapanasati). Not panting, but deep inhalations that fill the lungs, followed by a long, relaxing exhalation. This oxygenates the brain and "wakes up" the body.
    - **The "Reset" Button:** Whenever anxiety arises, the first action is always to take a few deep breaths. This is the primary tool to shift the mind away from the reptilian brain's fear response and toward a calm, energized state.

3.  **Understanding Anxiety as Chemistry:**
    - Anxiety is a set of inappropriate chemicals (cortisol, adrenaline) from our ancient "reptilian brain." These were useful for physical danger, but are now misfiring in our safe, modern environment.
    - By breathing deeply, we switch from the "danger chemicals" to "pleasure chemicals" (dopamine on the in-breath, serotonin on the out-breath). This creates a state of *Sukkha* (pleasure/well-being) and a "winner's attitude."

4.  **The Cycle of Practice (Right Sati & Right Effort):**
    - **Sati (Mindfulness):** Catch the mind wandering. The moment you notice, say "Aha! I caught you!" with gladness, not frustration.
    - **Action:** Immediately take a deep breath to re-energize and re-center.
    - **Result:** Come back to a state of pleasure and clarity. This is the "first Jhana" state—a fit mind for working with hindrances.
    - **Crucial Point:** The goal is not to *avoid* anxiety forever, but to have *Sati* available so that when anxiety arises, you are in a state of pleasure and can look at it as a "toy" rather than being overwhelmed by it.

5.  **The Danger of "Good Ideas" and Planning:**
    - The student's habit of looking ahead and planning the day is a form of distraction that feeds anxiety. The teaching is to **stop having good ideas** and to simply be present in the moment. "Be here now" (Ram Dass) or "Thusness" (Tathagata) is the state to cultivate.

**Final Outcome:**

The student experiences a significant shift during the conversation. He moves from feeling confused and anxious to a state where he feels "it's very alive." He realizes he doesn't need to know what to do for the rest of the day; he can just take it moment by moment. He understands that the feeling of "less like myself" is actually a step toward "no self" (Anatta). The practice is simplified to: **Every time you remember, take a deep breath with gladness, and choose to stay in the present moment.**
