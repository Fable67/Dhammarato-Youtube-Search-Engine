6.  **The Moon is a Harsh Mistress (Speaker B)**
    - **Story:** Speaker B references the science fiction novel by Robert A. Heinlein. He mentions the phrase "TANSTAFFL" (There Ain't No Such Thing As A Free Lunch) from the story, where lunar prisoners use a computer to calculate trajectories to catapult rocks back at Earth.
    - **Purpose:** To introduce the idea that the "no free lunch" mentality is a product of a competitive, protective mindset, rather than a universal truth. It is contrasted with compassion and teamwork (donuts).

7.  **Attachment Styles Book (Speaker A)**
    - **Story:** Speaker A summarizes a book he read about adult relationships based on John Bowlby's attachment theory (secure, anxious, avoidant).
    - **Purpose:** To connect psychological concepts to the Dhamma, noting that he identifies as "secure," but acknowledging that people can move between these states depending on the situation.

8.  **The Father and the Grass Cutting (Speaker B)**
    - **Story:** Speaker B recalls his father calling him "lazy" for not cutting the grass. He reframes this: "If he wanted it cut, why didn't he cut the grass?"
    - **Purpose:** To challenge the concept of "laziness" as a label applied by others, distinguishing it from "procrastination," which involves aversion and anxiety.

### Other Notable Figurative Language

- **"The whole idea of Zen and the Art of Donuts" (Speaker B)** : A metaphor for creating a friendly, team-oriented atmosphere to reduce competition and fear in the workplace.
- **"The Path has to be walked" (Speaker A)** : A common Zen/Buddhist metaphor for the necessity of practice and direct experience, rather than just intellectual understanding.
- **"Living on borrowed time" (Speaker A)** : Referring to the temporary relief provided by his eczema medication, framing it as a limited window in which to change his habits.
