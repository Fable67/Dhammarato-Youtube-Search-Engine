### Summary of the Dhamma Talk on "Dasa Nusati" (Ten Recollections)

**Main Topic:** The talk focuses on **Dasa Nusati** (the Ten Recollections), a set of meditation themes for reflection. Dhammarato emphasizes that these are not abstract concepts but practical, internal tools for cultivating a happy, noble, and easy life.

**Key Clarifications:**
- **"Dasa"** can mean "ten" or "servant." Here it means "ten."
- **"Nusati"** means to reflect, recollect, pay attention to, or spend time with.
- The original suttas list six, with four added later, but Dhammarato treats them as a complete practice.

**The Ten Recollections (as explained):**

1.  **Buddha Nusati:** Reflecting on the qualities of the Buddha—specifically that he taught only about *Dukkha* (suffering) and the end of *Dukkha*.
2.  **Dhamma Nusati:** Remembering and applying the Eightfold Noble Path.
3.  **Sangha Nusati:** Recollecting and cultivating noble, Dhamma friends. This is the very act of being on the call together.
4.  **Sila Nusati:** Reflecting on one's own virtue (e.g., "It's been a long time since I've harmed anyone"). This brings pleasure and confidence when feeling low.
5.  **Caga Nusati (Generosity/Gratitude):** Recollecting both one's own generosity towards others and the gratitude one feels for what is received (e.g., from friends).
6.  **Devata Nusati (The Core of the Talk):** **Reflecting on your own "devahood."** Dhammarato clarifies this is **not** about heavenly beings or metaphysics. "Deva" means a high-quality, noble, lucky, fortunate person (like a celebrity, a king, or a well-raised rich man's son). It is the feeling of being an "A-plus human being." The practice is to feel **lucky, worthy, and that everything is good enough.**
7.  **Anapanasati (Mindfulness of Breathing):** Added later. Dhammarato says this is essentially the practice of "gladdening the mind" and becoming a "deva" (full of *sukkha* and *piti* - ease and joy).
8.  **Kaya Nusati (Body):** Reflecting on the body's comfort. If the body has everything it needs, it reinforces the feeling of being a Deva (everything is taken care of).
9.  **Marana Nusati (Death):** Reflecting on life's impermanence. This encourages one to live in a high-quality, worthwhile way *now*.
10. **Upasama Nusati (Peace/Nibbana):** Reflecting on peace, relaxation, ease, and Nibbana—a state of "no problems, no worries."

**Core Teaching:**
- **Overlap:** All ten recollections overlap significantly. One reflection (e.g., Devata) can encompass many others.
- **The Essence:** The entire practice can be boiled down to remembering that **you are okay, you are a good person, and life is easy.**
- **Relationship to Dukkha:** The practice involves investigating mind states to see if there is Dukkha. If there is, you leave it; if not, you rejoice ("Whoopee!"). The goal is not to dissect Dukkha but to see it and avoid it.

**Practical Advice:**
- Spend time regularly reflecting on these themes, especially **how fortunate and easy your life is.**
- Cultivate noble friendships, as this is a core part of the path (Sangha Nusati).
- Don’t get lost in metaphysical speculation about "devas"; instead, feel the quality of being a "high-class person" in the here and now.

**Closing:** The talk ends with a reminder to "take it easy, take care, and don't worry, be happy," followed by announcements about the Sangha Discord, website (opensanghafoundation.org), Dhammarato's books, and a searchable archive of talks.

    - **Usage:** Speaker A states, "I'm in heaven. I live in paradise." He then ties it to the physical pleasure of eating: "Upaya salad, that's paradise. Paradise for the mouth."

### Stories

1.  **Prajņa's Reflection on Bendabout:**
    - **Story:** Speaker A recounts a phone call with "Prajna," who while "out on Bendabout," reflected on how fortunate he felt to be in the Sangha.
    - **Purpose:** This story is used as a direct example of practicing *Devata Nusati*—feeling lucky, worthy, and that "everything is taken care of." Prajņa’s key reflection, "I made that shit up," is quoted to illustrate that problems are self-created.

2.  **Speaker A's Personal Recollections:**
    - **Story of Lost Friends:** Speaker A tells a story about traveling and losing touch with almost everyone he knew before his 30s, except for "Ajahn Po."
    - **Story of Cultivating Friendships:** He follows this with a story of how he has "been keeping better track of who are my friends" for the past 10 years, using "Garantha's" first call 6 years ago as a milestone.
    - **Purpose:** These stories serve to illustrate the importance of *Sangha Nusati* (recollection of the Sangha) and of actively cultivating noble friendships.

3.  **The Story of "Mikey" and Generosity:**
    - **Story:** Speaker A mentions how he "used to give Mikey money on a regular basis," but now Mikey has a job.
    - **Purpose:** This is used to illustrate the practice of *Gatha Nusati* (recollection of generosity and gratitude). The playful addition, "I wish he'd get fired so I could be more generous to him," highlights the active desire to be generous.
