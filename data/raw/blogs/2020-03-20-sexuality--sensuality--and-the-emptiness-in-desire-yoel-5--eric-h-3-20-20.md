### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk given by Dhammarato with Sangha friends.

**Core Topic:** The nature of sensual desire (specifically sexual desire) as a mental hindrance, and the practice of using wisdom (Sati) to overcome it, leading to a deeper understanding of emptiness (Sunyata).

**Key Points:**

1.  **Sensual Desire is Mental, Not Just Physical:** Dhammarato reframes "sensual desire" as a form of **materialism**—wanting what we don't have. He explains that human sexuality has shifted from an olfactory (smell-based) drive in animals to a primarily **visual and mental** one in humans. The problem isn't the physical sensation but the **mental image and the story we create around it**, which leads to craving.

2.  **The Hindrance as Suffering:** The core issue is that wanting something you don't have is inherently **painful (Dukkha)** . The initial pleasurable "hit" of dopamine from a thought or image quickly turns into dissatisfaction and craving for more. Recognizing this painful wanting is the moment to "wake up."

3.  **The Practice (Sati & Wise Action):**
    - **Catch It:** Become alert to the hindrance the moment it arises ("I caught you").
    - **Gladden the Mind:** Instead of chasing the object of desire, find joy and satisfaction in **being free** from that hindrance. This success itself provides a "hit" of well-being (serotonin), which is more stable and wholesome than dopamine.
    - **Make a Choice:** You have choices. The old advice to "think of something else" is wise. The goal is to feel complete and satisfied in the present moment, rather than seeking fulfillment through an external object.

4.  **The Deeper Teaching on Emptiness (Sunyata):**
    - The feeling of **emptiness** that drives craving is actually a profound insight. People spend their lives trying to "fill the void" (with relationships, possessions, etc.), but it never works.
    - Using the metaphor of a **cathedral (Basilica)** : The "self" is like a vast, empty cathedral. It is not a hole to be filled; it is a **playground to be enjoyed**. Trying to fill it with objects (desires) only creates obstructions.
    - True freedom is found in **letting go** and **dwelling in the void**. This emptiness is a state of peace, not lack. It allows you to release the boundaries of the self and see the bigger picture (Noble Right View).

**Practical Advice:** When a sensual thought arises, don't get lost in the story. Recognize the suffering of wanting, take joy in your freedom from that particular desire, and explore the spacious, empty quality of your mind as a place of peace and satisfaction, not a void that needs filling.

### 1. Metaphors for Sensual Desire and the Shift from Olfactory to Visual

- **"Dog Love" (Butt Smelling):** This is a central metaphor used to contrast human sexuality with that of other mammals. The speaker explains that for most mammals (dogs, camels, "hairy creatures"), primal sexuality is driven by **olfactory** senses (smell). The metaphor is used to highlight that humans have shifted from this ancestral, instinctual mode to a **visual** and **mental** mode. The speaker says: *"Humans have gotten away from the olfactory into visualization, that that's where almost all of our sexuality is."*

- **The Female Breast as an Object of Beauty:** The speaker uses the human female breast as a key story/example to illustrate this shift. He notes that in other mammals, the breast is only present during nursing, but in humans, it has become a permanent object of visual and tactile beauty. He argues that this is not about the physical sensation itself ("the tactile part is in fact the same") but about the **imagination** and **conceptualization** that surrounds it.

- **The 7-Eleven Girl Story:** A specific, relatable anecdote is used to demonstrate how desire arises in a modern context. A man sees a "beautiful girl" behind the counter. The speaker explains that the man then creates a **story** and **conceptualizes** her in his mind, based entirely on visual input. He emphasizes that the man doesn't know anything about her (e.g., what she smells like), highlighting how human desire is now a mental, conceptual process.

### 2. Metaphors for the Mechanism of Desire (Dopamine) and Mental Suffering

- **"A little shot of dopamine" and "That's its own hit of dopamine":** The speaker uses the chemical dopamine as a metaphor for the pleasurable feeling of anticipation and wanting. He explains that a thought or image creates a "little hit," but the mind wants "more and more and more of that dopamine." The metaphor is used to describe the cycle of craving (*Tanha*) and the insatiable nature of desire.

- **The Parking Space / Receptor Metaphor:** To explain why the pleasure of desire fades (e.g., with pornography addiction), the speaker uses a physical metaphor: *"It's like one parking place, and that dopamine molecule is already there, so there's no more room for the uptake of the dopamine."* This illustrates the law of diminishing returns and why a person needs more and more stimulation to get the same effect.

- **The Cathedral / Basilica (from a quote of Jesus):** This is the most detailed and central metaphor for emptiness. The speaker references Jesus's saying "The kingdom of God is within you." | **Dopamine / Parking Space** | Law of diminishing returns in craving | The pleasure of wanting fades, leading to obsession and addiction. |
| **The Cathedral (Basilica)** | Emptiness (*Śūnyatā*) as a positive quality | The "inner emptiness" is not a void to fill, but a sacred, freeing space. |
| **You ain't seen nothing yet** | Misunderstanding of emptiness | People haven't yet realized that emptiness is a desirable state of being. |
| **General in a Hot Air Balloon** | Noble Right View | Rising above details to see the whole strategic picture of the mind. |
| **The Empty Sala (Hall)** | Emptiness as "what is not there" | Progress on the path is measured by the increasing absence of defilements. |
