### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato (Speaker A) and a student (Speaker B).

**Core Theme:** The talk focuses on shifting from a "victim" mindset to a "winner" mindset through the cultivation of **Noble Right View** as the foundation of the Eightfold Path.

**Key Points:**

1.  **Language as a Reflection of Mindset:**
    - Dhammarato points out the student's repeated use of the word "try." He explains that "try" is the "victim's favorite word" because it implies an expectation of failure and a desire for credit without full commitment. He encourages the student to be mindful of speech and to adopt the language of success.

2.  **Two Kinds of Right View:**
    - **Ordinary (Mundane) Right View:** This is necessary for society to function (e.g., morality, rules). However, it is based on fixed rules and superstitions, and it "ripens in clinging," meaning it still leads to suffering because it is tied to selfishness.
    - **Noble (Supramundane) Right View:** This is flexible, investigative, and not fixed. It is based on understanding **cause and effect (Paticcasamuppada)** . The key is not jumping to conclusions but maintaining an attitude of "I don't know yet; let me investigate." This view leads to wisdom and liberation.

3.  **The Core Triad of the Path:**
    - Dhammarato explains that **Right View, Right Effort, and Right Mindfulness** run in a circle around each other.
    - **Right View:** It is better to have a mind free from hindrances (greed, aversion, doubt).
    - **Right Sati (Mindfulness/Remembering):** Remembering that the mind *is* in a hindrance.
    - **Right Effort:** The effort to clean the hindrances out.
    - The result of successful effort produces a **"Can-Do Attitude"** (the attitude of a winner, not a victim).

4.  **Working with Feelings:**
    - The practice involves becoming wise to feelings. When a feeling arises (like anger from someone else), we can choose how to respond instead of reacting ignorantly. If we can control our breath and throw out hindrances, we can learn to control feelings and not be pulled into them (e.g., not getting angry just because a child is having a tantrum).

5.  **Investigating the Self:**
    - Noble Right View is achieved by deeply investigating the **Four Noble Truths**, especially the Second Noble Truth (the cause of suffering). This investigation reveals that we are not who we thought we were, and it is through seeing the chain of cause and effect in the mind that we find freedom.

**Takeaway for the Student:**
- Stop "trying" and start *doing*.
- Cultivate a flexible, investigative mind that doesn't fixate on conclusions.
- Use the practice to clean the mind of hindrances and develop the unshakable confidence that you can do it.

### Metaphors and Stories

1.  **"The victim's favorite word" (Try):** The word "try" is framed as a verbal signal of a defeatist mindset. The metaphor positions the speaker as a "victim" who expects to fail and uses the word to get credit for effort without genuine commitment.

2.  **"Being a lion" vs. "being a victim":** This is the central metaphor for shifting one's identity and attitude. The "lion" represents a winner, someone with a can-do attitude who takes control. The "victim" represents passivity and helplessness.

3.  **"Right View comes first" (as a driver):** Right View is described as the "driving force" of the Eightfold Noble Path, establishing it as the foundational engine that powers the rest of the practice.

4.  **"A cosmic clock tick":** Used to describe the sequential, moment-by-moment nature of cause and effect (dependent origination). Each tick marks a moment where an effect becomes the cause for the next event.

5.  **The Domino Effect:** A clear, visual metaphor for the chain of cause and effect (dependent origination). Just as one domino falling causes the next to fall (becoming a new cause), so too do mental events unfold in a causal sequence.

6.  **"The mind works exactly like that, like a string of dominoes":** This extends the domino metaphor to the internal mental process, showing how ignorance leads to feelings, which lead to craving, which leads to suffering, all in a linked causal chain.

7.  **"Simple arithmetic" (2 + 3 = 5):** A metaphor for compound causality. It illustrates that sometimes multiple conditions (like ignorance and a feeling) must combine to produce a specific effect, rather than a single cause.

8.  **"Seeing dots and learning how to connect them":** Describes supramundane right view as the wisdom to perceive how events are moving and where they are heading, based on understanding the conditions of the present.

9.  **"Run a circle around each other":** Describes the three core path factors (Right View, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness) as a dynamic, interdependent loop, where each supports and reinforces the others.

10. **"Bathing in it" (clouded):** Speaker B uses this metaphor to describe being completely immersed in and overwhelmed by Wrong View, like being submerged in a murky substance.

**Stories/Illustrative Examples:**

1.  **The personal anecdote (from 1975):** Speaker A shares his own history of learning about the word "try" around 1975, illustrating how he began to monitor his speech to align it with a more noble, less victim-like mindset.

2.  **The story of the angry person at the bank:** An example of how someone with an ignorant intention (wanting to spread anger) can be met with wisdom. The wise person, by being aware of their own feelings, can choose not to adopt the other person's anger.

3.  **The story of the 4- or 6-year-old child having a tantrum:** This contrasts the typical ignorant parental reaction (getting angry back, threatening) with a wise one. The wise parent, by controlling their own feelings, can "tease the child" and "get them to smile and laugh again," changing the emotional dynamic.

4.  **The story of the office meeting:** An illustration that even eyewitnesses to the same event have different, changing, and incomplete views of what happened. This serves to support the supramundane right view of "I don't know" and not clinging to a fixed conclusion.

5.  **The example of scientific researchers:** A model for the practice of supramundane right view. Scientists who test an experiment "over and over and over again" before publishing are used as an analogy for how one should test their own mind and insights before coming to a final conclusion.
