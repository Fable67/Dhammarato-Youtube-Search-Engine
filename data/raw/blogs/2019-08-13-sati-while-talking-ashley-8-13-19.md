    - *Narrative 1 (The Well):* The King of Thailand came to Wat Suwan Mok to **dig a well** with his own equipment.
    - *Narrative 2 (Refusing Elite Status):* Bhikkhu Buddhadasa **refused** the high rank of "Chao Khun" (equivalent to a cardinal) which elevated his status even higher.
    - *Narrative 3 (Refusing Royal Treatment):* When close to death, the King offered the royal helicopter to take him to a top hospital. Bhikkhu Buddhadasa **refused**, saying he would die right there.
    - *Lesson:* These stories demonstrate the "lion" or "champion" quality. True spiritual authority (based on Dhamma) can stand equal to or above worldly power (kings, generals) and is not swayed by status or comfort.

4.  **The Story of the Recovering Drug Addicts / "The Ladies" video:**
    - *Narrative:* Referenced indirectly. The Speaker A had a student who ran a rehab program and was told to stop calling the patients "girls" and start calling them "ladies."
    - *Lesson:* It illustrates the power of language and respect in social interaction. The simple act of changing a title changes the relationship and the dignity offered to the other person.

5.  **Anecdote of the Grieving Dog Owner:**
    - *Narrative:* A woman who had just lost her dog came to Speaker A. Instead of commiserating in silence, Speaker A started talking about a cute dog he once saw and how silly it was. He "confronted" the grief with joy.
    - *Lesson:* This is a practical example of the "joy is contagious" principle. The role of the mindful person is not to sink into the other's suffering, but to skillfully sprinkle joy into the conversation to lift them up.
