### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk given by Dhammarato and the Sangha:

**Core Topic:** The talk focuses on **Samma Sanghappa**, which Dhammarato translates as both **attitude** and **intention**. He explains that these two concepts are intertwined: every attitude contains an intention, and every intention contains an attitude.

**Key Distinctions & Concepts:**

- **Three Pali Words:** Dhammarato distinguishes between *Sankhara* (the act of piling on), *Sankhata* (the pile itself), and *Samskara* (both the piling and the pile). This leads to the idea that our intentions are the force that piles things onto our mind.
- **Right View vs. Wrong View vs. Ordinary View:**
    - **Wrong View:** A nihilistic, bully-like view (winning/losing, life/death).
    - **Ordinary View:** An eternalistic view (rules, reincarnation, a soul).
    - **Right Noble View:** This is a **verb**, not a noun—it is **"right viewing"** or investigating in the present moment. It is not about believing in rebirth (which Dhammarato argues is a mistranslation of *jati* meaning "something new" happening now).
- **The Path is a Verb:** The Eightfold Noble Path is composed of actions happening *right now* (viewing, intending, changing), not static concepts.
- **Victimhood to Confidence:** The default human attitude is that of a **victim** (dependent, bullied). The practice is to change this to a **winner’s attitude**— "I've got this," "Hold my beer," "We can do this."
- **The Missing Link:** Attitude is the muscle that determines everything. If you have a lean (like a leaning tree), your thoughts and actions will fall in that direction. The practice is to train the attitude directly.

**The Practice (Anapanasati):**

1.  **Remember** to look.
2.  **Look** (investigate) at the current state of mind.
3.  **Change** (gladden the mind) from unwholesome to wholesome.
4.  **Nourish** this new attitude continuously to prevent falling back into old habits.

**Samadhi (Right Unification):**
- This is **not** concentration (narrow focus).
- It is **open awareness**—being alert, unified, and aware of everything inside and out. You cannot be snuck up on. The purpose is to see *Dukkha* (suffering) pop up so you can change it.

**Group Discussion & Q&A Highlights:**
- **Eric** shares that he is building the "attitude muscle" and has been happier in the last 6 months than in his adult life.
- **Gigi** learns to "drop the humbleness" and not make humility an identity that stops her progress.
- **Red** discusses the story of the monk with a leaky roof, and Dhammarato clarifies that the point is *not* to fix the roof, but to fix your **attitude** about the roof (whether it's raining or not).
- **Mikey** closes by reminding everyone that the "hole in the roof" is the one in your mind where *Dukkha* rains in. The practice is about being okay regardless of external conditions.

**Overall Takeaway:** The entire practice of Buddhism, according to Dhammarato, boils down to actively developing a **noble intention/attitude** in the present moment. It is a mental muscle that, when trained, allows you to sit on top of your world and be satisfied, regardless of external circumstances.

### Metaphors and Stories

Based on the talk provided, here are the metaphors and stories used by **Speaker A (Dhammarato)** :

### Metaphors

1.  **The Library and The Piles of Books (Sankhara, Sankhata, Samskara)**
    - **Sankhata (The Pile):** A library where all the books have been pulled off the shelves and piled into the middle of the floor—uncatalogued, just a "pile of junk."
    - **Samskara (The Piling On):** The act of donating new books to the library and piling them onto the existing pile. This represents both the intention and the action of accumulating.
    - **Sankhara (The Total Process):** Both the pile (the result) and the piling on (the intention/attitude).

2.  **The Leaning Tower of Pisa / Leaning Tree**
    - Attitude is compared to a building (vertical = good attitude; leaning = bad attitude).
    - **The Direction of Fall:** A leaning building or tree will fall in the direction it is leaning, just as a person's thoughts and actions will follow the direction of their attitude/intention.
    - **Guy Wires:** Force is required to change the direction of a tree that is already leaning, similar to the effort required to change a habitual attitude.

3.  **The Bully and The Victim**
    - **Wrong View:** Described as a "bully, dictator, or Revolutionary Guard" who compensates for their own victimhood by pretending to be stronger than others. Their worldview is about "winning and losing."
    - **Ordinary View:** Described as the "victim" (the person who fears the bully). The speaker argues that everyone starts life as a dependent victim (baby) and that this victimhood grows (victim of teachers, police, IRS, bullies) unless it is actively changed.

4.  **Sports and Music**
    - Sports is described as a field where people "inadvertently work with attitude" (e.g., "I've got this," "We're on top of the world").
    - Music is contrasted; most musicians have a "bad attitude" and their music, while beautiful, is often "sad."

5.  **The Zen Master's Stick**
    - Used to illustrate the difference between **concentration** (the common translation of *Samadhi*) and **unified/open awareness**.
    - The Zen master hits the person who is *too focused* (concentrating), not the one who is alert and aware. A concentrated person can be "snuck up on," but a person with open awareness (like Ajahn Po) cannot.

6.  **The Emperor of the Pile of Dirt**
    - Everyone is the emperor or king of their own "pile of dirt" (their personal world/experience).
    - One can choose to "wallow in the dirt" (victimhood) or "sit on top of the world" (mastery/right attitude). No struggle is required; you can "just pop right up" because you are already in charge.

7.  **Attitude as a Muscle**
    - Attitude and intention are not fixed traits but are developed like a mental muscle.
    - Most people have an "underdeveloped" muscle or have developed it in the "wrong direction."

8.  **The Leaky Roof (Story from the Suttas/Discord)**
    - This story is used to argue that the problem is not the external condition (the leaky roof) but the **internal attitude** toward it.
    - The monk can fix the roof (with a happy or unhappy attitude), sit in a dry corner, or leave the hut. The core teaching is that attitude dictates the experience, not the roof.
    - **Mikey’s extension:** "There's a hole in your roof where Dukkha is raining on yourself. Fix up that hole."

9.  **Gravity / Velocity**
    - Used by **Speaker D (Brett)** : "We have escaped velocity from the gravity well of the Sankharas." This frames the practice as a launch, escaping the pull of conditioned habits and wrong intentions.

### Stories (Anecdotes and Examples)

1.  **The Smile & The Frown (Greg and JK)**
    - An in-the-moment demonstration of *rebirth* (Jati) as defined by the speaker.
    - **Speaker A** says the word "smile" and Greg smiles. He then asks JK to look, and that person's expression changes. The story illustrates that "rebirth" is not a future life but a constant moment-to-moment arising of new states based on conditions (e.g., the speaker's words).

2.  **The Translators (Tanisaro & Bhikkhu Bodhi)**
    - An anecdote about *why* the Pali word *Jati* (meaning "something new") is mistranslated as "rebirth."
    - The speaker claims these translators deep down "want to believe in eternalism" or "reincarnation" and their scholarship is based on past beliefs rather than immediate investigation of the present moment.

3.  **Eric’s Personal Practice**
    - A shared personal story (by **Speaker C**) about recognizing a "shitty attitude" in the body and mind, learning to train it like a muscle, and reporting that the last 6 months have been "the happiest in my adult life."

4.  **The Misunderstood Child's Attitude**
    - The speaker contrasts the two types of attitudes parents see in children: the "bad attitude" (resistance) and the "nowhere attitude."
    - This contrasts with the Buddhist goal of developing a "positive, on-top-of-things attitude."

5.  **The Casinos (Life and Death)**
    - Used to illustrate Wrong View (Nihilism). When a person feels they are losing, they feel like they are dying. Casinos are described as being "all about life and death" because they trigger this deep-rooted sense of annihilation.

6.  **The Atom (From Greek *Atomos*)**
    - A scientific/metaphysical story to illustrate "no soul" (*Anatta*).
    - The word atom means "uncuttable," but physicists were "surprised" to find atoms could be cut. The speaker uses this to say there is no permanent, inherent "thing" (no soul) to be found.
