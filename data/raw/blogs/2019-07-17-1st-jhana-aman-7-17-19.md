### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato (Speaker A) and a student (Speaker B):

### Core Topic: Mastering the First Jhana and Building Rapture

The student describes entering a pleasant, stable meditative state but losing it quickly. Dhammarato explains this state is likely **First Jhana** and provides a detailed framework for entering it, stabilizing it, and using it for deeper work.

**Key Points of the Talk:**

1.  **The Analogy of Making Dough/Biscuits:** The mind is like scattered flour. The "drops of water" are the factors of Jhana (gladdening the mind, freedom from hindrances) that you add repeatedly. When enough drops are added, the mind solidifies into a unified, workable "ball of dough" (First Jhana).

2.  **The Missing Ingredient: Vedana (Feeling):** The student was missing the crucial factor of **Vedana** (emotional feeling/relationship between body and mind). Most meditators train themselves to feel bad when they get distracted. Instead, one must train in **good feelings**.

3.  **The Jhana Factors (Piti & Sukkha):**
    - **Piti (Rapture):** This is the "thrill of winning." It's the intense, buzzing, tingly sensation of success. Dhammarato emphasizes that this is what all humans chase (gambling, winning arguments, getting contracts). In meditation, it comes from the success of catching the mind wandering. You must celebrate this victory intensely.
    - **Sukkha (Satisfaction/Bliss):** After the intense "in-breath" of Rapture, you relax into a deep, beautiful "out-breath" of Satisfaction. This is a state of complete contentment, safety, and ease.

4.  **How to Enter First Jhana:**
    - Use **Anapanasati** (mindfulness of breathing).
    - First, get free from hindrances.
    - **Gladden the mind:** When you catch a distraction, don't be disappointed. Say "Aha, I caught you!" and celebrate that success.
    - Add **Vedana**: Bring in the *feeling* of that success. This intense, joyful feeling is the Piti.
    - Let that Piti melt into **Sukkha** (Satisfaction).

5.  **What to Do in First Jhana (Mind Fit for Work):**
    - **Watch Thoughts (Citta Sankhara):** Guard the mind. Refuse entry to unwholesome thoughts (past, future, wanting). You are satisfied, so you just stay.
    - This leads to **Second Jhana**, where thoughts stop entirely, and you feel a deep "bubbling and churning" (like an artesian well) of underlying mental/emotional formations.
    - **Third Jhana** is when you become strong enough to be unaffected by that bubbling (like a lotus flower in a fast stream).

6.  **Core Advice for the Student:**
    - **Stop mapping and start developing.** Don't get lost in intellectual analysis of which Jhana you were in.
    - **Bring intensity.** Don't meditate weakly. Deliberately generate the feeling of *intense success* and *deep satisfaction*.
    - **Change your attitude.** Stop "sucking" on life (wanting it to give you something). Become satisfied, and life stops sucking. You are the winner.

**Final Takeaway:** The path to freedom is learning to manufacture the feeling of success (Rapture) and then relaxing into deep Satisfaction (Sukkha). This is the foundation for all deeper insight and emotional purification.

### 3. The Artesian Well / Pond Metaphor (for Second Jhana)
- **The Metaphor:** Speaker A describes a pond formed around an artesian well (a natural spring). The cold water bubbling up from the bottom mixes with the warmer surface water.
- **The Point:** This describes the experience of the Second Jhana, where thoughts have stopped but the mind is still "bubbling and churning" with subtle energy (mental formations or feelings). The meditator is like a swimmer sitting at the source of the spring, feeling the intense, overwhelming flow of this "churning" from within the body-mind.

### 4. The Fast-Flowing Stream / Lotus Flower (for Third Jhana)
- **The Metaphor:** Speaker A briefly describes the Third Jhana as a "fast-flowing stream" where the "lotus flower" is completely submerged by the current.
- **The Point:** This represents the overwhelming force of the feelings (Citta Sankhara) in the Second Jhana. The lotus flower being "strong enough to stand up to that current" and becoming "dry" signifies the mind gaining strength over these formations, becoming unaffected by them. This is the process of tranquilizing the mental formations, leading to equanimity.

### Summary of the Core Narrative ("Story"):
The overarching story told here is a **journey of transformation from victim to winner**.
- **The Problem:** The story begins by describing the human condition. People are "sucking on life" for a thrill, feeling "not good enough" due to cultural and religious conditioning (the "poison of the culture"). Life "sucks."
- **The Method:** The Buddha offers a path. The student is instructed to change their attitude by using *Vedana* (feeling). Instead of feeling bad for wandering, they should feel the "thrill of winning" for noticing.
- **The Reward:** By using this method (the water droplets), one builds a "ball of dough" (First Jhana) and becomes like the successful runner. The student is told they are "not going to feel the way they used to feel" when attacked. They become the "winner" who has "the very best thing that life has to offer."
- **The Buddhist Critique:** A minor story is inserted critiquing religions (specifically Christianity) for making people feel bad (sin) so they have something to sell, and critiquing meditators who mistake a good feeling for "God" or "enlightenment" rather than seeing it as a state to be developed and used.

In essence, the talk uses the concrete, physical act of **cooking/baking** (making biscuits) and the emotional thrill of **competition/winning** (racing, gambling) to explain the abstract, internal process of mindfulness leading to Jhana.
