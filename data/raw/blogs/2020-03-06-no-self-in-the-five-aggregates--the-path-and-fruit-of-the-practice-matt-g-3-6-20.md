**2. The First Fetter: Personality View (Sakkaya-ditthi)**
- This is the central topic. The self (or ego) is identified as the root cause of suffering (Dukkha). It arises from grasping and clinging (Tanha/Upadana) and manifests as "selfishness" and a sense of "me vs. the world" (e.g., "this line is not fair to *me*").

**3. The Problem of Wrong View & Belief Systems**

**4. The Path to Stream-Entry (Sotapanna)**
- **Dhammarato** outlines the stages of realizing the first three fetters (personality view, doubt, attachment to rites & rituals):
    - **Step 1:** The knowledge that no matter how obstructed the mind is, it can be cleared.
    - **Step 2:** The first Jhana (getting the mind fit, calm, and pleasant).
    - **Step 3:** The knowledge and vision of what is and is not the path, which eradicates doubt.
- This leads to the **"fruit of Sotapanna"** (the downstream path after the tipping point). This is characterized by a new dedication:
    - **Step 4:** Willingness to see one's own wrongdoing and be corrected by others (giving up protecting the "non-existent self").
    - **Steps 5 & 6:** Growing dedication and eagerness for the Dhamma.
    - **Step 7:** **Delight in the Dhamma** – a joyful confidence that one has the tools to handle life and sidestep suffering. This is the "budding of the lion," a joyful and capable practitioner.

**5. The Core Takeaway**
- The entire practice is about using **Sati (mindfulness)** to see the arising of the self (the cause of suffering) and letting it go. The litmus test is simple: the self is always associated with selfishness and dissatisfaction. The path is not about following rules, but about developing a skill that leads to fearlessness, ease, and genuine joy, even in difficult circumstances.
