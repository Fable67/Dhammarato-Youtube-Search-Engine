### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato (Speaker A) and a student (Speaker B):

**Core Theme:** The student reports a significant shift in his relationship with depression—from a constant state to shorter, manageable episodes. Dhammarato identifies this as a key milestone on the path: **the arising of confidence and the "supramundane" knowledge that one can emerge from any mental obstruction.**

**Key Points from the Discussion:**

1.  **The Turning Point (Confidence):** The student describes how depression now lasts for weeks, then days, then hours. Dhammarato emphasizes that this isn't just improvement; it's the emergence of a **"can-do" attitude**—a noble factor of the path where the student *knows* they can wake up and work out of a state, rather than being lost in it.

2.  **The Mechanism (Breath & Right Effort):** Dhammarato stresses that the first step out of depression is **deep breathing**. He explains that depression dulls the mind (oxygen deprivation) and activates the "reptilian brain." Deep breathing re-oxygenates the frontal cortex, allowing clarity. This is the first "right effort."
    - The second right effort is **gladdening the mind**—being pleased that you're breathing and working with the state.

3.  **Depression & Anxiety as Two Sides of the Same Coin:** The student experiences both. Dhammarato clarifies that restlessness/boredom (common in depression) and anxiety are essentially the same underlying agitation, perceived differently. Recognizing this unity helps in working with them.

4.  **The "Letting Go" Experience (Cold Shower):** The student shares a powerful experience where, feeling stuck in a fake "right attitude" (imposing "I can do this" on himself), he gave up and said, "I can't do it, you win." By staying in the cold shower anyway, he discovered that **accepting the unpleasantness ("this is fine") dissolved the struggle** more effectively than fighting it or using forced positivity.

5.  **The Value of Sangha (Spiritual Friends):** The student credits his weekly Sangha in Seattle, saying it gives him a "hit of confidence" even when he arrives depressed. Dhammarato affirms that this is "Kaliyametta" (noble spiritual friendship), which the Buddha said is "the whole of the holy life."

6.  **The Danger of Dreaming vs. Reality:** The student notes that success has led to having many ideas and plans. Dhammarato warns against the trap of "dreaming" (future planning) at the expense of present reality, noting that many ideas are just magic that keep us from being satisfied with "this is good enough right now."

7.  **Spreading the Dhamma:** The student finds great joy (a "high") in helping a friend with his suffering simply by embodying right attitude and listening. Dhammarato relates this to his own path, acknowledging the natural desire to share the teachings once you see their value, but also the need for support (lineage).

**Final Advice from Dhammarato:**
- **Add more air** (deep, conscious breathing).
- **Add more joy and satisfaction**—treating it as a skill to be developed.
