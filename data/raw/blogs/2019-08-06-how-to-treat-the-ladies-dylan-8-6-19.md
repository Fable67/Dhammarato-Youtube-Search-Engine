### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato (Speaker A) and a Sangha friend (Speaker B):

**Core Theme:** The talk focuses on correcting common misunderstandings in Buddhist practice, emphasizing direct, immediate experience over striving for future attainments.

**Key Points from the Discussion:**

1.  **Jhana is Not an "Attainment":** The speaker clarifies that the first Jhana is defined by the presence of its five factors (like moving past hindrances, experiencing *piti*/rapture, and *sukkha*/pleasure). He strongly criticizes the Western Buddhist tendency to view Jhana as a "magical attainment" or a prize to be grasped. Instead, Jhana is a state of immediate satisfaction and success that requires letting go, not clinging to a "super-duper self."

2.  **Immediate Gratification vs. Struggling:** The student describes intentionally trying to enjoy the breath, which works immediately. Dhammarato praises this as "wholesome volition" because the cause and effect are instantaneous. He contrasts this with the wrong effort of struggling now to get results later, which is based on concepts and expectations, leading to disappointment and clinging.

3.  **Advice for the Goenka Retreat:** The student asks for tips on an upcoming 10-day Goenka retreat.
    - **Build Community:** Befriend the staff and long-term residents at the center.
    - **Listen Carefully:** Follow the instructions closely, especially Goenka’s tapes. Dhammarato suggests his own teachings are like "frosting" on Goenka’s "cake," so the student should look for the consistency rather than getting confused.
    - **Appreciate the "Donation" Model:** Dhammarato strongly praises Goenka's free-by-donation model. He contrasts it with centers that charge fixed fees, arguing that charging turns the Dhamma into a commodity and prevents the teacher from embodying the security and generosity they are supposed to teach.

4.  **Applying Dhamma to a New Job (Women's Rehab):** The student works in a state-run drug rehab for women on court order and asks about handling boundaries compassionately.
    - **Be on Their Side:** The boundaries are set by the judge, not the student. The student’s job is to be on the *women’s side* of that boundary, helping them navigate it with joy and friendship, just as they are stuck with it.
    - **Prioritize a Good Heart:** Rather than policing rules, the student should be a "compassion and joy machine," helping the women build bonds with each other (modeled on AA) and using humor (*mudita*).
    - **Handle Romantic Attention:** A woman is attracted to the student. Dhammarato gives the teaching of Ajahn Chah: "I like it, but I don't want it." The student must first straighten out his own mind to not want the relationship, as any hint of wanting will be seen and exploited. Once clear, rejection can be done playfully and compassionately (e.g., teasing, redirecting their love toward each other).

5.  **The Purpose of Jhana (First vs. Higher Jhanas):** The student asks about moving to higher Jhanas. Dhammarato advises against it for now. The purpose of first Jhana is **insight**—it’s the "sweet spot" where you can observe the mind working. Higher Jhanas shut down thinking, which can be a form of bypassing or escape. The goal is to maintain first Jhana as a stable platform for wisdom, not to chase "higher" states out of lust or wanting.
