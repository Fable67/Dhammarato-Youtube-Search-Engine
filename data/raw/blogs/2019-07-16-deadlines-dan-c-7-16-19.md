### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato and Dan (Speaker B):

**Core Theme:** The talk focuses on the difference between seeking happiness and a "sense of accomplishment" from external events (like meeting a grant deadline) versus *training the mind* to generate those feelings of success, joy, and satisfaction from within, using the practice of *Anapanasati* (mindfulness of breathing).

**Key Points:**

1.  **The Mistaken Source of Happiness:** Dan is disappointed about missing a grant deadline. However, Dhammarato points out that the real loss isn't the money or the grant itself, but the **feeling of victory, success, and "the juice"** that Dan was hoping for. The deadlin he has artificially created this suffering by tying his happiness to an external event.

2.  **Turning the Mind into a Winner:** Dhammarato emphasizes that one doesn't need an external "win" to feel good. The practice is to cultivate the **attitude of a "lion" or a winner** directly in the mind. Instead of feeling like a victim of circumstances, one should recognize that the real victory is in the ability to control one's own mental state.

3.  **Applying Anapanasati:** Dhammarato advises using the meditation steps (specifically steps 5-8 of Anapanasati) to practice this:
    - **Piti (Rapture/Joy):** This is the exhilaration of success. Dan should practice being intensely happy and excited about the *act of catching the mind* or being mindful, rather than waiting for a grant to feel that way.
    - **Sukha (Happiness/Satisfaction):** This is the relaxed sigh of contentment that follows the rapture. This is the steady state of being satisfied with the present moment, independent of worldly outcomes.

4.  **Working with Thoughts:** Dan notes that his suffering is linked to negative verbal thoughts (simulating arguments, explaining things to others). Dhammarato agrees and says this is the "same old Samsara." The practice is to see these unwholesome thoughts arise and then, with the intensity of a winner, throw them out and return to the joyful present moment. Every time a hindrance arises, it's another opportunity to "dance" and celebrate winning.

5.  **Intensity vs. Boredom:** Dan mentions getting bored with practices like using a clicker. Dhammarato suggests that the issue isn't the object of meditation, but the lack of **intensity**. Instead of mechanically clicking to "be here now," he advises doing a full, intense cycle of Anapanasati (with Piti, Sukkha, and guarding the mind) with each click, turning it into a powerful, joyful 10-second exercise.

6.  **Deadlines are Artificial:** Dhammarato reframes deadlines. They are not the cause of happiness or suffering. Joy comes from the mind's ability to be present and victorious. He encourages Dan to see that suffering is a choice, and he can "pick himself up, dust himself off, and boogie on down the path" of the Eightfold Noble Path.

7.  **Monastery vs. Lay Life:** When Dan asks if monastic life is easier, Dhammarato explains that both worlds have their own challenges and mental turmoil. The key insight is "wherever you go, there you are." The *opportunity* to wake up is what matters, not the external environment.

**Conclusion:**

The ultimate teaching is: **your happiness and success are a skill to be developed, not a result of external outcomes.** Dan must stop waiting for the NIH or a deadline to give him the feeling of victory. Instead, he should practice generating that "winning feeling" (Piti and Sukkha) directly in his mind through Anapanasati, with intensity and a lion-like attitude, every moment he remembers to do so.
