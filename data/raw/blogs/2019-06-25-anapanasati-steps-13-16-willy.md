### Summary of the Dhamma Talk

This talk covers the beginning of **Dhammanupassana** (contemplation of Dhamma), specifically steps 13-16 of Anapanasati. The core teaching is about understanding the nature of reality as a vast, interconnected, and uncontrollable process of cause and effect.

**Key Points:**

1.  **Vipassana is "Investigation," not just "Insight":** The speaker clarifies that "Vipassana" literally means "in-seeing" or a deep investigation, not just a sudden flash of insight.

2.  **You Are the Dhamma (Non-Duality):** The word "Dhamma" has multiple levels.
    - **Low level:** A "thing" or object.
    - **Medium level:** The contents and processes of the mind.
    - **High level:** The entire universe, the "big It."
    - The key teaching is that **we are not separate from the Dhamma; we are the Dhamma.** We are as much a part of the universe as the chair we sit on. The electromagnetic forces holding us up are the same as those in the chair. This points to **non-duality** and profound interconnectedness.

3.  **The Universe is a Process of Constant Change & Causality:** The Buddha saw what modern physics is now discovering: reality is a constant, blindingly fast flux of cause and effect (Paticcasamuppada).
    - Everything is constantly changing (*anicca*).
    - Nothing is in control of itself. Every single cause is also an effect of something else.
    - The "self" is an illusion created by desire, grasping, and clinging. The physical body, feelings, and consciousness itself are all dependently arisen processes with no permanent, independent self (*anatta*).

4.  **Step 13: Seeing the Flux:** This stage of practice is about beginning to recognize that everything is "in a flood," constantly tumbling and interacting. Nothing sits still; everything is a process like a cosmic tumbleweed.

**Practical Instructions for the Listener (Willie):**

- **Observe the Mind's Cycles:** The listener notes his mind cycles through dullness and brightness. The teacher affirms this is part of seeing the flux.
- **Use Deep Breathing for Dullness:** When dullness arises in meditation, take deep breaths to re-energize the mind (up to the point of true physical tiredness).
- **Investigate Causality (Nutriment):** Study the concept of "nutriment" (*ahara*). Think of everything as "food" for something else. If the cause (nutriment) is present, the effect happens; if not, it doesn't.
- **Let Go of the Self:** Since the universe is a process with no self and no controller in charge of anything, the only appropriate response is **relinquishment**—letting it all go.

**Final Takeaway:**

The speaker summarizes the entire teaching: **"There is no self in the universe anywhere, and there's no self possible in a human being."** Everything is just an interaction of causes and effects, constantly in flux. The goal is to see this reality directly, understand that nothing is in charge, and practice letting go.

### Metaphors and Stories

Based on the transcript, the speaker uses several vivid metaphors and stories to explain the concept of Dhamma (Dharma), non-self (Anatta), and the nature of reality. Here is a breakdown of the key metaphors and stories used:

**1. The Fish and the Ocean (Metaphor)**
- **What it illustrates:** The relationship between the individual and the "big Dhamma."
- **Quote:** "like a fish who swims in the ocean doesn't know that he is in the ocean. But fishermen think that the fish is in the ocean, but the reality is that the fish is the ocean."
- **Meaning:** We are not separate from the Dhamma (the universe, the law of nature); we are a part of it and made of it. The separation is an illusion.

- **What it illustrates:** Interconnectedness at a physical/quantum level.
- **Quote:** "the reason that you're sitting on the couch... Without electromagnetic... gravity would pull you right through the chair... You are the couch you're sitting on."
- **Meaning:** The boundary between "you" and the couch is an illusion. They are interacting through electromagnetic forces. The body is not a solid, separate entity but a process in relationship with everything else.
