### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato and Sangha friends (Steve):

**Overview:**
This is a coaching session where Steve, a mechanic and meditator, shares his practice progress with Dhammarato. The talk focuses on navigating the ups and downs of meditation, understanding bodily sensations, and using daily life (especially work) as a practice ground. Dhammarato gives Steve a new, practical assignment to deepen his wisdom.

**Key Points from the Conversation:**

1.  **Progress & Hindrances:**
    - Steve reports progress in his practice (35 min sits twice daily, full-day seclusions).
    - He experiences periods of clarity, happiness, and mental sharpness.
    - However, he is currently facing a resurgence of hindrances, particularly **doubt** and **sloth/tiredness**, and a difficult "worst sit" in a while.

2.  **Understanding Bodily Sensations ("Shimmering"):**
    - Steve describes feeling his body "shimmering" or "flickering."
    - **Dhammarato's Teaching:** These are normal, real, internal sensations that we usually ignore. The mistake is to interpret them as external or magical (guru's blessing, religious experience). The correct view is that you are simply becoming more sensitive to what is already happening inside the body.

3.  **The Core Teaching: Mindfulness of Feelings (Vedana):**
    - This is the main "assignment" for Steve.
    - **The Practice:** Moment-by-moment, notice the immediate mental feeling-tone that arises with every experience: **"I like it," "I don't like it," or "I'm not sure."**
    - **Why it matters:** This is the hinge point of Dependent Origination (Paticcasamuppada). If you are mindful of the feeling ("I like it") *without* automatically reacting, you can stop the chain from going into craving ("I want it"), clinging, and suffering.
    - Example: At the dentist, Steve noticed attraction to the dentist. The teaching is to recognize "I like looking at her" and stop there, rather than moving into "I want" and potential suffering.

4.  **Work-Life & The Hindrances:**
    - Steve struggles with his busy, high-volume auto body repair business.
    - **Dhammarato's Suggestion:** Use the "I like it/I don't like it" framework at work. Notice the feeling when you pick up a tool: do you like this tool? Do you like this job, or just the money?
    - A tool-based mindfulness exercise: Pick up each tool, remember where it came from, a job it helped with, and **thank it**. This changes your relationship from frustration to gratitude.
    - Steve is considering transitioning from high-volume trade work to lower-volume, higher-quality retail work, and is even planning to pass his business to a student.

5.  **The Nature of "I":**
    - Steve observes that his sense of self is starting to change.
    - **Dhammarato's Refinement:** Instead of thinking "I am changing," the wiser view is "This thing called 'I' is a process that is constantly changing." The real "me" is the process that gives rise to the thought "I."

6.  **Comparison and Jealousy (Mana):**
    - Steve discusses the negative reactions (jealousy) he gets from people due to his perceived success.
    - **Dhammarato's Insight:** Avoid the trap of comparing yourself to others entirely. If you compare and win, you get pride. If you compare and lose, you get jealousy. Instead, practice "giving it back" to the other person by asking about them. You see people crave feeling good; you can help them feel good without boasting.

7.  **Generosity and Gratitude:**
    - Steve is generously supporting his girlfriend so she can quit her job and start a creative business.
    - **Dhammarato's Teaching:** True generosity is not about the amount, but the value received by the other person. Giving directly (putting in a well yourself) creates a joyful feedback loop of gratitude. Giving money to an NGO is hollow and impersonal.

**Overall Takeaway:**
The talk provides a practical, real-world meditation instruction. The core lesson is to use the simple framework of **"I like it / I don't like it / I don't know"** as a constant, moment-to-moment mindfulness tool. This cuts through the automatic reaction (craving/aversion) that leads to suffering, breaks the power of conditioned habits, and leads to a wiser, more skillful engagement with life.
