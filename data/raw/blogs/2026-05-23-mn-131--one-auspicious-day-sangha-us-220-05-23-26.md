### Summary of the Dhamma Talk

The talk centers on **MN 131: "One Auspicious Day"** (or "One Fortunate Attachment"), which instructs practitioners not to chase after the past or place expectations on the future, but to see the present clearly without being taken in by it.

**1. The Core Teaching (No Past, No Future, No Self in the Present)**
- The sutta teaches that we should not dwell on past experiences (what we had or were) or fantasize about the future (what we might become).
- The key to practice is seeing the present moment clearly, specifically the **five aggregates** (form, feeling, perception, formations, consciousness).
- Dhammarato emphasizes **Anatta (not-self)** : The aggregates are not who you are. You should not see them as self, or self as possessing them.

**2. A Key Philosophical Point (Contra Ajahn Thanissaro)**
- Dhammarato argues that the Buddha taught **"no self anywhere" (Sabbe Dhamma Anatta)** .
- He critiques Ajahn Thanissaro’s interpretation that leaves room for a "self" or soul to justify rebirth.
- **Rebirth vs. Reincarnation:**
    - **Reincarnation** requires a "soul" (an Atta) that travels from life to life (like a Social Security number tracking data).
    - **Rebirth** (as invented by later Buddhists) also implies a causal link that, without a soul, is scientifically and metaphysically impossible.
- The Buddha’s silence on the question of a self (with Vacchagotta) was not an opening for a soul; it was him saying the questioner was not ready to understand.

**3. Practical Application (Staying Present)**
- **Present Moment:** The goal is to stay in the present. If you’re thinking about Iran (news) or politics, you’re living in the past.
- **Planning vs. Obsession:** It’s okay to make a simple plan (e.g., "I will buy furniture"), but do not obsess over it. The right time to think about the plan is when you are actually executing it.
- **Avoiding "Wholesome" Cliches:** Even a seemingly positive thought like "everything is okay" can be a trap if it is immediately followed by an unwholesome doubt like "but it’s not satisfying enough."

**4. The Solution: The Mentor Program (Sangha)**
- The cure for falling back into past/future thinking is the **Sangha (community)** .
- Dhammarato compares this to the **AA (Alcoholics Anonymous) mentor program**: The mentor’s job is to keep the new member "sober" (i.e., in the present, with a gladdened mind).
- **Hierarchy of Mentors:**
    - Everyone needs a mentor.
    - While friendship is good, a formal commitment to keep someone "sober" in the Dhamma is required.
    - Senior practitioners (Alex, Agnes, Ben) may not need a daily mentor, but are available to mentor others.
    - **Dhammarato** offers to be the mentor for the mentors themselves.

**5. Call to Action**
