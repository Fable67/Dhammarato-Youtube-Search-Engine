### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato and the student (Speaker A):

**Core Issue:**
The student has been struggling with instability and "stupid energy" (coarse *piti*/excitement) from mouth-breathing and intensive Vipassana techniques. He wants a simpler, more stable path.

**Key Teaching Points from Dhammarato:**

1.  **The Simplicity of the Practice:** The student does not need to focus on nostrils, building *nimittas* (visual signs), or chasing the breath in the body to get to first *Jhana*. The only necessary practice is **long, slow, deep, rhythmic breathing** (in and out, through the nose if possible). This is enough to settle the mind.

2.  **Purpose of First Jhana vs. Higher Jhanas:**
    - **First Jhana** (the "sweet spot") is all that is needed to make the mind "fit for work" (investigation). It provides calm without inducing passivity.
    - **Higher Jhanas** (like 2nd Jhana) are for *Arahants* who have no more work to do; they are for pleasure, not for beginners trying to solve suffering.

3.  **The True Goal is Investigation, not Trance:** The student should not just sharpen the sword (build deep concentration). Once the mind is settled (First Jhana), he must put it to work by investigating the **Four Noble Truths**:
    - *This is suffering* (including the agitation/excitement he feels).
    - *This is the cause* (the wanting/aversion).
    - *This is the end of suffering*.
    - This leads to understanding *Paticcasamuppada* (dependent origination) and how the mind wrongly processes sensory input.

4.  **Correction on Text:** The book (*Anapanasati* by Buddhadasa) includes *nimittas* and advanced steps because it was written for experienced monks, not beginners. The student should skip those technical details and focus on the long breath.

5.  **The "Never Mind" Approach:** The essence of the entire practice is simple: When the mind wanders, say "Never mind, start again," and return to the long, slow breath. No complex techniques are needed.

**Practical Advice for the Student:**
- **Breathe through the nose.** It stabilizes the system and cools the agitation; mouth-breathing creates a coarse, excited *piti* (tension) that mimics bliss but is actually stressful.
- **Focus on the "in and out" of the breath as a concept**, not the nostrils. This prevents tension and blocked sinuses.
- **Use the settled mind to look at suffering** (his own agitation, desires) rather than trying to escape into oblivion or higher pleasures.

**Conclusion:**
Dhammarato redirects the student from chasing technical, pleasure-based states (nimittas, deep jhanas) to a foundational, simple practice (long breathing) aimed at clear investigation of *Dukkha*.
