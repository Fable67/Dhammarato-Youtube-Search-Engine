### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato and the Sangha friend (Speaker B):

**Core Problem: The "Two-Position" Trap**
Speaker B describes a common meditation problem: when they stop deep breathing to calm the mind, they immediately fall back into suffering and "feeling like shit." Dhammarato explains this is because most students only know **two positions**:
1.  Being in a hindrance (suffering).
2.  Being out of a hindrance (neutral/ordinary).

The problem is that a neutral state is weak. It has no momentum, so the mind easily slips back into old habits of suffering.

**The Solution: The Third Position (Joy & Satisfaction)**
The missing piece is the **third position**: a state of **satisfaction, joy, and success**. You cannot just "stop" suffering; you must actively replace it with a wholesome, joyful feeling. This is the essence of Right Effort (not hard effort).

**Key Teaching Points:**

- **Right Effort vs. Wrong Effort:** Western students tend to "work too hard" (leading to neck tension, fatigue). Right Effort is noble and refined. Instead of mucking around in the muck (like dealing with asbestos), you want to be the "front office"—the boss who is satisfied and in control.
- **The Goal is Internal Joy:** You must learn to manufacture joy (Piti) within your own mind. If you cannot do this, you will forever be a beggar looking for satisfaction from the outside world (materialism, sports teams, status, etc.). The "hole" inside you cannot be filled by external things.
- **Being a Winner:** You must adopt the attitude of a winner, a lion, a noble person. Use thoughts of success ("I can do this," "This is marvelous") to generate the corresponding feeling. The joy of "winning" the battle against your own wandering mind is greater than any Olympic gold medal.
- **The Three Feelings:** Liking (leads to wanting/owning), Disliking (leads to suffering), and the **Third Feeling** (confusion/not-knowing). This third feeling is the most dangerous, leading to doubt, fear, and restlessness. You must learn to bypass this by choosing joy.
- **Anapanasati & Satipatthana:** The practice is not "choiceless awareness" (letting the mind spray bullets everywhere). It is **choosing** your frame of reference. Use the breath as an anchor, but actively gladden the mind (Step 10 of Anapanasati). Recognize when the mind wanders (Step 9/13) and **relinquish** the hindrances (Step 16).
- **The Bouncer Analogy:** You must become the bouncer at the door of your own mind. Do not let unwholesome thoughts in that will disturb your state of satisfaction. If one gets in, grab it and throw it out immediately.
- **First Jhana & Nibbana:** The goal is to enter and maintain the **First Jhana**—a high-quality state of satisfaction and success. This makes the mind "fit for work."
    - **The "Work":** The only work is to *guard* that state of satisfaction. Not to fight, but to be on guard.
    - **Nibbana:** Being completely satisfied and content *right now* is the end of the search. If you are truly satisfied, you don't *want* enlightenment. That satisfaction is Nibbana itself—the coolness of not wanting.

**Final Takeaway for Practitioner:**
Instead of trying to suppress suffering or just letting the mind float, **actively cultivate the feeling of success and joy.** Remember a time you felt like a champion (e.g., winning an award). Use that memory to generate Piti. Take a deep "winner's breath" and then sigh with relief. That state of satisfaction is the entire path. It is "really chill" but requires the skill of learning to be happy on purpose.
