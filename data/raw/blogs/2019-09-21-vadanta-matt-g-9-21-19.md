### Summary

Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato and the Sangha friend (Speaker B):

**Core Question:** The speaker asks whether the Advaita Vedanta concept of "everything is Brahman" (oneness) and the Buddhist concept of "interbeing" (dependent origination) are essentially the same teaching. He also wonders if the goal of *Sat-Chitta-Ananda* (being-consciousness-bliss) is the same as *Nibbana*.

**Dhammarato's Key Points:**

1.  **Historical Cross-Pollination:** The traditions are not separate, pure lineages. They grew up together, influencing each other. Advaita Vedanta (Shankara) is very close to Buddhism and was criticized as "crypto-Buddhist."

2.  **The Core Experience is the Same:** Both traditions point to the same **direct, non-conceptual experience of the present moment**. The problem is that most people experience the "Big Brahma" or "Dhammakaya" only as a *concept*, not as a *reality*.

3.  **What the Experience Is:** It is **"objectlessness"** – opening the senses to the flood of reality without focusing on any single object, classifying it as safe/dangerous, or creating a conceptual "virtual reality." This state is characterized by **awe, rapture (*Piti*), and enormous satisfaction.** This is the state the Buddha called himself *Tathagata* (the one who is "thus-gone," or "here-now").

4.  **The Danger of "Experience":** Having a one-time mystical experience is not the goal (that is just "beginner's luck"). The goal is to **develop the skill** to enter this state anytime, using the systematic path the Buddha laid out.

5.  **The Path (Paticcasamuppada & Anapanasati):**
    *   The map is **Dependent Origination (*Paticcasamuppada*)**. We practice by working *backwards*: catching clinging, then intention, then feeling, then the mental creation process.
    *   The vehicle is **Anapanasati (mindfulness of breathing)**. The different teachings (Four Foundations, Seven Factors, Eightfold Path) are all the same thing, just using different language.
    *   The key practical skill is monitoring **Citta Sankhara** (the emotional/heart mind). Feelings color our perception and create suffering if we let them run the show.

6.  **Suffering as a Teacher (The Cow Pie Analogy):**
    *   The First Noble Truth is not a statement that life is suffering, but an invitation to **see suffering clearly** (like seeing a cow pie).
    *   The Second Noble Truth says **you** made the mess. You are responsible for watching where you step.
    *   The Third Noble Truth is dropping the conceptual world and diving into the flood of sensory reality.
    *   **Skillful Engagement with Pain:** You don't suppress or indulge feelings (like sadness or anger). You investigate them, make friends with them, but **you remain the Emperor of your own pile of dirt.** They do not pull you off your seat of satisfaction.

**Practical Homework (for the next talk):**

*   **Shift Focus:** Stop worrying about the "map" of feelings in meditation. Instead, **develop the skill of feeling like a champion.**
*   **Cultivate the Feeling:** Get in touch with the feeling of being successful, powerful, and satisfied. "What does it feel like to be Brahma? Thou art that. Work on that feeling."
*   **Use Real Life:** Use parenting (like dealing with a child's tantrum) as the practice ground. Instead of reacting with anger, remain in a state of satisfaction and respond with wisdom (e.g., humor).

**Conclusion:** The speaker agrees to explore the intersection of *Anapanasati* and feelings (*vedana*) in the next talk, but Dhammarato reframes the assignment: Don't just *study* feelings; *cultivate* the feeling of being a satisfied, successful lion (the Buddha) right now.
