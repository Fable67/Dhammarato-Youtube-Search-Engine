The student reports a significant shift: they no longer feel conflicted between "the Dhamma" and "the world." They can practice Dhamma while being in the world, feeling less bothered by suffering and less inclined to run from it.

**Key Points from the Discussion:**

1.  **Suffering vs. Self-Preservation:** The default human mode is self-preservation, which is rooted in the mistaken belief that the "self" needs protection. This very drive *is* suffering. The goal is to shift focus from protecting the self to understanding suffering itself.

2.  **Catching Yourself:** When you notice your own "bad behavior" (e.g., an angry email), rather than hiding from it or fighting it (guilt), you should be grateful. Seeing your own faults is a lesson. This is like the first meditation instruction: when the mind wanders, you don't fight it; you just start again.

3.  **Not My Business (NMB):** This is a key slogan. It means avoiding the "fruit of the knowledge of good and evil"—i.e., not making constant judgment calls (good/bad, right/wrong). The less you judge, the less you suffer. It's a way of "not placing a bet" on the outcome of the world.

4.  **Conceit and Being Special:** The need to be "special" is a deep layer of conceit, used to protect the self from the "horror" of being ordinary. This is the source of anxiety and competition.

5.  **The World (Samsara) is Corrupt:** Dhammarato gives a long, cynical view of the world (using examples from politics, insurance, and industrial psychology). It is inherently corrupt and set up to benefit the few. You cannot fix it. Trying to "climb the pyramid" only leads to moral decay and exhaustion.

6.  **Nibbana in Samsara:** The solution is not to escape the world (you can't) or to try to fix it. The solution is to find the "hub of the wheel." The world spins around you, but you don't have to spin with it. You become the calm center. This is "being in the world but not of it."

7.  **Letting Go of Pushing & Pulling:** The student realized they had been pushing their musical career away as a reaction to previously pulling it toward them. The answer is to stop both pushing and pulling. Just "be here now." If the desire to play arises, play. If not, don't. There is no need for grand plans or harsh rejections.

8.  **Mind is Foremost:** Actions don't matter as much as the mind. You can't be "right" all the time, but you can be happy. The goal is to dig up the suffering in your mistakes and see the "pony in the horseshit."

9.  **Practical Skill:** You can't clean the street, but you can watch where you step. You can't change the visa officer, but you can empathize with their constraints and skillfully jump through the hoops required by "outrageous Samsara."

**Conclusion:**
The student has moved from internal conflict (push the world away) to a place of stability. The practice is now about staying at the hub, watching the world spin, and skillfully navigating the "shit on the floor" without getting caught in the wheel of self-protection, judgment, and conceit.

### Metaphors and Stories

Based on the transcript, here are the key metaphors and stories used in the talk, categorized for clarity:

**Major Metaphors:**

1.  **The Wheel & The Hub (Samsara & Nibbana):**
    - **The Wheel** represents *Samsara* (the cycle of suffering, the corrupt world, the daily drama).
    - **The Hub** represents *Nibbana* (inner peace, unification of mind, the stable center).
    - **The Spokes & Rim** represent being caught up in the turmoil, spinning out of control, or grinding oneself into the dirt by being on the edge.
    - **Speaker B:** *“What is called the unification of mind. When the mind becomes unified, it's like it becomes the hub, right? Rather than spinning around as part of the wheel, somewhere on one spoke or another, or out there on the rim.”*

2.  **The Snake & The Tongue:**
    - **The Snake** represents the dangerous, corrupt world (Samsara).
    - **The Tongue** represents the safest place to be—inside the snake’s mouth, because it won’t bite itself. This illustrates how to live *in* the world without being harmed by it.
    - **Speaker B:** *“The drawing of a snake with a monk inside the mouth of the snake. And the caption is, Be like the tongue of the snake. Well, the snake's not going to bite his own tongue... the safest place to deal with that snake is in his mouth.”*

3.  **The Swamp & The Alligators:**
    - Refers to the futility of trying to fix the corrupt world.
    - **Speaker B:** *“Every crusader who wanted to fix the world has gotten swallowed by the alligators in the swamp, or becomes one of the alligators himself.”*

4.  **The Pyramid (of Success/Conceit):**
    - Describes the struggle for status and specialness. The person at the top is constantly being pulled down.
    - **Speaker B:** *“The pile of humans trying to climb to the top of the pyramid, because only the guy at the top has the real understanding of how special he is... now there's 9 hands on his trousers pulling him down.”*

5.  **The Dinner Plate:**
    - A metaphor for one's livelihood or passion (in this case, drumming). It is a simple, practical thing to be enjoyed, not an object of conflict.
    - **Speaker B:** *“Besides, I said the drums is your dinner plate, right? And you enjoy your students.”*

**Key Stories & Anecdotes:**

1.  **The Optimist & The 55-Gallon Drum of Horse Manure:**
    - A story about a pessimist (given all the toys) and an optimist (given a barrel of manure). The optimist digs happily, saying, "With all this horseshit, there's got to be a pony in there someplace."
    - **Moral:** Find the potential good (the "pony") even in difficult or messy situations, or simply let the pony come to you without sifting through the shit.
    - **Speaker B:** *“With all this horseshit, there's got to be a pony in there someplace.”*

2.  **The Buddha's Teaching on the Mind vs. Action:**
    - Referencing a *sutta* where the Buddha corrects Brahmins who valued action over speech and thought.
    - **Moral:** The mind is the forerunner; thoughts and feelings are the root causes, not just external behaviors.
    - **Speaker B:** *“The Buddha turned that upside down and says, oh no, the mind is the fore-runner... The real issue is the mind.”*

3.  **Adam and Eve (The Fruit of Knowledge):**
    - Used as a metaphor for making binary judgments (good/evil, right/wrong). Eating the fruit is the *result* of making these judgments, not the cause. It illustrates "Not My Business" (NMB).
    - **Speaker B:** *“The story of Adam and Eve is the story of Adam and Eve having to eat the fruit of their knowledge of having made those kind of judgment calls.”*

4.  **Stockholm Syndrome & Patty Hearst:**
    - A real-world story about a wealthy heiress who was kidnapped and eventually joined her captors in robbing banks.
    - **Moral:** Illustrates how inheriting wealth can "train" one to be an asshole, and how the corrupt system of "big money" blinds people to reality.
    - **Speaker B:** *“Inherited wealth is nothing but a 20-year-long childhood training in how to grow up to be an asshole.”*

5.  **The Visa Office Anecdote:**
    - Speaker B’s experience with Thai retirement visas and his student’s experience with Russian visas.
    - **Moral:** The "slings and arrows of outrageous Samsara" (like bureaucratic red tape) cannot be fought. One must empathize with the agent, dodge the obstacles, and not take it personally.
    - **Speaker B:** *“The best thing to do is to watch out for it, be aware of it, know when it's coming, and have the skill to jump over it.”*

**Minor Metaphors & Concepts:**

- **Fight or Flight vs. Wisdom:** Rejecting the reptilian instinct to hide from or attack suffering, choosing instead to observe it (the "third option").
- **Not My Business (NMB):** The concept of not judging outcomes or placing bets (like in a casino), thereby avoiding the suffering of being wrong.
- **Being in the World but Not of the World:** A direct quote from Jesus, used to summarize the entire talk's goal of inner peace amidst external chaos.
- **Dodging the Shit on the Floor:** A practical, gritty metaphor for navigating daily life without trying to clean up the whole mess.
