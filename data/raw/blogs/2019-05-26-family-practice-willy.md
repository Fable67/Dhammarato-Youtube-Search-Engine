5. **The Piece of Meat / Cornered Animal** (Speaker B, referenced by A)
   - The instinct to hide one's wallet when giving is compared to an animal wanting to eat "in the corner" with its meat.

6. **The Demon** (Speaker A)
   - When referring to the father's fear of being forsaken: "this is a demon that needs to be thrown out."

7. **The Baseball Game / Second Inning** (Speaker B)
   - The new phase of practice (spreading joy) is compared to "the second inning of this baseball game."

8. **Cold & Rational vs. Hot & Stupid** (Speaker B)
   - Mindfulness as "cold and rational" vs. the alternative being "hot and stupid."

9. **Normal but Not Natural** (Speaker B)
   - Describing the human condition: "It's normal behavior... but it's really not the natural way to live. Natural way to live is happy."

## **Stories**

1. **The Metro North Sit** (Speaker A)
   - Describes getting 45 minutes of meditation on the train, and experimenting with not using a timer.

2. **The Timer Anxiety** (Speaker A)
   - Story of the mind worrying 10 minutes before the timer should go off ("Is the phone not working?").

3. **The Grand Central Homeless Encounter** (Speaker A)
   - Describes seeing multiple homeless people, skipping three, and giving a dollar to one while in a rush. Admits to judging ("look at him, he's got a dog").

4. **The Wallet Avoidance** (Speaker A)
   - Realizing that giving is easier when bills are already out of the wallet, due to the instinct not to show money in public.

5. **The Brother's Criticism** (Speaker A & B)
   - Speaker A's brother called mindfulness "cold and rational" years ago. Speaker B reframes it as "cool and rational" vs. "hot and stupid."

6. **The Dentist and the Monk** (Speaker B)
   - Story of a Laotian monk having a tooth removed without anesthetic, focusing on keeping his hands relaxed during the procedure.

7. **The Blood Draw** (Speaker A & B)
   - Both speakers share stories of having blood drawn. Speaker B's vein "does not give up blood easily," and the nurse had to find an unusual spot. Speaker A describes looking away to avoid vein retraction.

8. **The Desert and the Bicycle** (Speaker B)
   - A story of being in a desert craving water, but keeping the mind focused ("one more pedal, one more pedal") to avoid suffering from the desire.

9. **The Ice Cream Trigger** (Speaker B)
   - Using the example of a friend mentioning ice cream after a hike to explain how desire arises from contact (phassa).

10. **The Zen Master's Therapist** (Speaker A)
    - Story of the father's therapist telling him to feel the chair, the sponge, and the water during daily activities—which Speaker A recognizes as "the Dhamma."

11. **The Apartment Argument** (Speaker A)
    - A detailed story about a conflict over moving furniture and auspicious dates. Speaker A inadvertently said "it's in my apartment," triggering the father's fear of being abandoned. Speaker A then had to repair the damage over the next day.

12. **The Dying Monk's Finale** (Speaker B)
    - Referring to terminal lucidity, Speaker B describes the "finale" of life, where wise people let go intentionally, while others cling and suffer more.
