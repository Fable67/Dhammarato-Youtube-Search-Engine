### Summary

This Dhamma talk between Dhammarato and a Sangha friend (Speaker B) explores the intersection of **Transactional Analysis (TA)** and **Buddhist practice**, particularly **Anapanasati (mindfulness of breathing)**. Here’s a concise summary of the key themes and insights:

### 1. **The Adult Ego State & Buddhism**
- **Speaker B** shares insights from the TA book *Born to Win*, which describes the **Adult ego state** as being in charge, choosing when to use the Parent (rules/superego) or Child (emotions/id). However, this adult responsibility can lead to "despair" when faced with genuine choice.
- **Dhammarato** reframes this: The TA "winner" is a lower, worldly win. True nobility (Ariya) comes from seeing **Dukkha (suffering)** and using the practice to **come out of it entirely**. TA stops short of complete liberation, but if practiced deeply (with breathing), it can lead to the Buddhist path.

### 2. **The Core Practice: Snapping Out of It**
- A key moment: **Speaker B** describes standing in a grocery store, torn between the Parent ("eat healthy") and Child ("don't want to cook"), and then **snapping out of it** with laughter, simply making a choice.
- **Dhammarato** celebrates this as **Sati (mindfulness) arising**—the frontal cortex waking up. The essence of the Buddha's teaching is: **"Look at what you're doing. If it's suffering, stop doing it."** This is not about trying harder, but about **seeing clearly** and choosing differently.

### 3. **The Role of Piti (Rapture/Success)**
- **Speaker B** describes a meditation where they felt fulfilled and "in flow," but it didn't last long.
- **Dhammarato** points out a missing factor: **Piti**—the feeling of "I can do this," the joy of *success* and *agency*. This is not just a pleasant feeling; it's the confidence that *you* are creating the state. He contrasts this with the old habit of feeling like a victim or a loser.
- **Key distinction**: *Gladdening the mind* (step 10 of Anapanasati) is a thought ("I am successful"), while **Piti and Sukkha** (steps 5 & 6) are actual *felt* experiences of that success.

### 4. **The "Winner's Attitude" & Skillful Means**
- Dhammarato emphasizes that you must **cultivate a "winner's" attitude** (the lion) to overcome the old "poor me" or "superego try-hard" patterns. This is a **skillful means**—using a thorn to remove a thorn.
- Eventually, even that identification with "being a winner" is dropped (as it is a form of conceit), but first you need it to **energize the mind** and build confidence in your practice.

### 5. **Practical Advice for Meditation & Life**
- **For meditation**: Focus on generating **Piti**—the feeling of "I can do this." Use **strong, deep breathing** to energize the mind. Once in a good state, **be on guard** for what pulls you out (investigate the "wanted posters" of your mind).
- **For therapy**: Dhammarato encourages **Speaker B** (a future therapist) to prioritize **being happy and sharing the Dharma** over being a "good therapist" by external standards. The core message is simple: **"Don't worry, be happy. Stop it."** Use pacing and leading (like the Buddha) to meet clients where they are.

### 6. **Final Point: Integration**
- The **unification of mind** (the goal of the path) is likened to the Adult, Parent, and Child working together, with the **Adult (mindful awareness)** in charge. This is the same as the **first Jhana**—a mind free from hindrances, fit for work, and vibrantly alive.

**In short**: The talk is a lively, practical guide on how to use the insights of TA (the Adult ego state) as a stepping stone to genuine Buddhist liberation, emphasizing **joyful agency (Piti)**, **mindful "snapping out" of old patterns**, and the **daily choice to be happy**.
