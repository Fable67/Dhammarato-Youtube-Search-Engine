Here is a summary of the Dhamma talk between Dhammarato and Speaker B (a student):

**Core Theme:** The student is experiencing a novel, pleasant "inner wind" sensation, particularly in the face and head, which correlates with periods of strong mindfulness. The teacher guides the student to investigate, understand, and skillfully work with this phenomenon rather than clinging to or rejecting it.

**Key Points of the Dialogue:**

1.  **Describing the Experience:** Speaker B describes a persistent "inner wind" or "surging energy" in the head and a feeling in the gut. Initially uncomfortable, it has become smoother and less bothersome. It is strongly correlated with, and seems to be an indicator of, good mindfulness.

2.  **Investigation & Classification:** Dhammarato guides the student to see this as a **milestone or indicator** (like a barometer) of practice, not the path itself. He suggests it may be a manifestation of **joy (*piti*)** or the energy of right effort being redirected from suffering to positive states.

The breath and sensation became one, leading to a feeling of wholeness. Dhammarato confirms this is **Samma Arya Samatha** (noble unified mind), not mere concentration. He warns **not to cling to the memory of this state** (which leads to suffering), but to investigate the factors that led to it and recreate them without desire.

4.  **The Trap of Wanting:** A major teaching point: **You cannot be "one with" something if there is a "someone" trying to be one with it.** Clinging or wanting the state pushes it away. The state is already present; it is the sense of "self" and "lack" that creates separation. Letting go of the desire *for* the state is the way to be *in* the state.

5.  **Technique for Mental Control:** To move from a "thought-ridden mind" to a "no-mind" state, Dhammarato introduces the concept of **limiting your "vocabulary"** (concepts, images, thoughts). This involves:
    *   Starting with **First Jhana**: Applying and sustaining the mind only on wholesome thoughts (e.g., investigating the Four Noble Truths in the present moment).
    *   **Narrowing the circle**: Shrinking the allowed concepts down to a "jingle" or even a simple mantra (e.g., "Budo").
    *   **The Breath Trick**: Using the out-breath as an opportunity to let the mind stop completely, holding the breath while holding no thoughts.

6.  **The Spiritual Disease & the Cure:** Dhammarato frames suffering (Dukkha) as a "spiritual disease" of dissatisfaction. The Dhamma is the "pill." The goal is to take the pill when sick (dissatisfied) and be well, rather than constantly striving for experiences.

7.  **The Ultimate Skill:** The central teaching is to develop the **confidence** that, no matter the state of the body or the mind (sick, exhausted, energetically overwhelmed), one can choose to remain happy and present.
