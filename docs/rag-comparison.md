The five factors of the first jhana are: [2][5]

1. Applied and sustained thought (keeping the mind on wholesome thinking and free from hindrances) [2]
2. Freedom from hindrances [3]
3. Piti (often mistranslated as "rapture") [4]
4. Sukkha (satisfaction, contentment) [3]
5. A relaxed body [5]

When these factors are brought together in samadhi, "that's the formation of the first jhana, which is the platform for which we're going to operate" [2]. The deeper jhanas (second through fourth) build upon this foundation [1].

The key indicator is that **all five constituent factors must be present together**. As stated: "If it is not wholesome, we're not in first jhana" [2]. 

Specifically, you know you are in first jhana when: [2][5]

- The mind is "free from the hindrances"
- You have "applied and sustained thought" keeping the mind on wholesome matters
- You experience Piti and Sukkha as developed skills
- The body is relaxed
- Everything you observe is "real and it is wholesome"

Importantly, experiencing only one factor—such as Piti alone—does not constitute jhana. "Even though you're feeling piti, that's not first Jhana. It's just one of the constituent components" [2]. Similarly, "going deep is what everybody thinks is Jhana...just doesn't have all the factors" [3].
